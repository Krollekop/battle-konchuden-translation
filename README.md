# Battle Konchūden — An English Translation Project

An AI-assisted translation project for Jaleco's 1999 Japanese PlayStation RPG.

## How it started

I've been fascinated by insects since childhood. I used to catch bugs in my grandma's garden, and I grew up playing Pokémon. Collecting creatures, learning about them, and discovering new species were things I loved long before I became interested in retro gaming.

Years later, discovering Sega's *Mushiking: The King of Beetles* in arcades brought back a surprising amount of those childhood memories. It felt like going back to my roots.

That fascination eventually led me to *Battle Konchūden* (バトル昆虫伝), a Japanese-only PlayStation game released by Jaleco in 1999. It's a simulation RPG built around insect battles, exploration, and collecting the insects you encounter.

I wanted to experience the game properly, but the Japanese text was a major obstacle. I couldn't find an existing English translation, so I decided to explore whether modern AI tools could help make one possible.

What started as curiosity quickly became a reverse-engineering and fan-translation project.

## What the project involved

The game turned out to be considerably more complicated than simply replacing Japanese sentences with English ones.

The work included:

- Investigating the disc structure and custom text formats.
- Reconstructing and rebuilding compressed dialogue data.
- Adapting the text system to accommodate English.
- Translating dialogue, menus, insect names, encyclopedia entries, and interface graphics.
- Correcting graphical text issues and checking text layout.
- Testing the translated game in an emulator, including the main story, final battle, ending sequence, and credits.

The project was completed with substantial assistance from AI tools, alongside iterative technical analysis and emulator-based testing.

## Current status

The main English translation has reached its final testing phase, and the main story has been played through to the ending credits in an emulator.

The principal patch passed its automated regression tests, and no new defects were found during the final story-completion phase.

Some areas remain incompletely tested, including later stages of the secret mode, certain item effects in Story Mode, and the optional PocketStation mini-game.

The original Japanese voice acting, game and studio logos, and selected decorative Japanese artwork have intentionally been preserved.

I'm hoping to make the patch available once the remaining checks are complete. In the meantime, I'd welcome feedback from people interested in retro gaming, obscure Japanese games, and fan-translation projects.

## Why document this project?

Part of what makes this project interesting to me is the possibility of using modern AI tools to explore older games whose data formats and text systems were never designed with fan translation in mind.

For me, the project also comes full circle: from catching insects in my grandma's garden as a kid, to growing up with Pokémon, to rediscovering that childhood fascination through *Mushiking* — and eventually finding a way to experience an obscure Japanese insect-battling RPG in English.

It's been a fascinating experiment in reverse engineering, translation, and testing, and a chance to bring a little-known Japanese game closer to an audience that otherwise might never experience it.
